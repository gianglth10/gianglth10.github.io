What I learnt in my first four months of SMU’s Master of IT in Business

A reflection on how returning to student life challenged my perspective, strengthened my foundation, and guided my growth in data and AI.

I still remember where I was around this time 6 months ago. I was overwhelmed by the workload at Shopee, but I kept encouraging myself to push through until my final working day. I looked forward to starting a new chapter and assumed that whatever came next could not possibly be as demanding as my experience at Shopee.

That was what I believed. Until one day...

My first week at MITB was relatively relaxing. I participated in as many activities as possible to immerse myself in the new environment. I wanted to experience student life again: a time when I could be carefree and focus solely on studying, without workplace stress, tight deadlines, or stakeholder management.

However, by the third week, I realised that it would not be as easy as I had imagined.

The lessons became increasingly advanced. During my first term, I took four modules: Algorithm Design and Implementation, also known as Algo; Statistical Thinking for Data Science, or Stats; Computational Thinking with Python; and Query Processing and Optimisation, which we often referred to as Query.

Each module offered a different perspective on how I understood, analysed, and used data in real-world applications. Query focused on how databases process information and make data retrieval faster and more efficient. Python introduced how programming could be used to implement solutions and translate ideas into code. Algo focused on designing efficient approaches and solving problems systematically, while Stats helped us understand how data could be analysed and interpreted using statistical methods rather than relying solely on human instinct.

Together, the four modules gave me a more complete understanding of how technical concepts could be applied to real-world business problems.

Query Processing and Optimisation

Initially, I thought Query and Python would not be major challenges for me. Since I had worked with SQL before, I assumed that Query would mainly be a revision of concepts I had already encountered in my professional experience.

However, the module surprised me. It was much more detailed and theoretical than I had expected. Instead of focusing only on how to write SQL queries, the course explored what happens behind the scenes when a database receives and processes a query.

I learnt how databases store information, execute operations, retrieve data, and optimise queries for better performance. Despite having relevant work experience, I still struggled with some of the theoretical concepts. Many of them were not topics I commonly encountered in my daily work, so I needed additional time to understand how the different components worked together. At the same time, this was what made the module valuable. It helped me connect my practical experience with formal concepts and terminology. It gave names and structure to ideas that I had previously understood only through hands-on work.

After taking the module, I became more aware that writing a working query was only one part of the process. Understanding its performance, efficiency, and scalability was equally important.

Computational Thinking with Python

Python was manageable at the beginning because the module provided a foundation for every MITB student, regardless of their previous programming experience.

The earlier lessons covered fundamental concepts such as data types, conditional statements, loops, functions, and basic problem-solving techniques. Since I already had some familiarity with Python, I initially felt comfortable following the lessons and completing the exercises.

As the term progressed, however, the topics became more challenging. We moved on to object-oriented programming, pattern recognition, regular expressions, bitwise operations, and more advanced algorithmic applications. These topics required a different level of logical thinking and attention to detail. Regular expressions were particularly challenging because a short expression could contain many rules and patterns. I needed to understand not only the syntax but also how to break a text-processing problem into smaller components. Similarly, object-oriented programming required me to think about how code could be organised into reusable and maintainable structures.

The module gave me the opportunity to review fundamental concepts and practise writing code more consistently. It reinforced my understanding of how different programming elements worked together and helped me become more comfortable applying them to a variety of problems.

Algorithm Design and Implementation

Algo was one of my weakest modules because I had no previous experience with the subject. I had not studied algorithms during my bachelor’s degree, and I had rarely needed to solve this type of problem in my previous work.

The module introduced me to different ways of designing algorithms and evaluating their efficiency. I learnt that there could be several ways to solve the same problem, but some solutions might require significantly more time or memory than others. When solving algorithmic problems, accuracy was not the only priority. We also needed to consider the efficiency of the solution.

Algorithmic problem-solving could not be mastered simply by memorising formulas or reading model answers. It required repeated practice. Sometimes, I could understand a solution after seeing it, but I would still struggle to develop the same solution independently.

I realised that I needed to dedicate more time to practising on platforms such as LeetCode. My goal was not only to perform better academically but also to strengthen my logical thinking and become more confident when facing unfamiliar technical problems.

Statistical Thinking for Data Science

Stats, on the other hand, was one of the modules I found most interesting. It refreshed many of the analytical concepts I had encountered before while helping me understand them in greater depth.

The professor explained the material clearly and comprehensively, making it easier to see how statistical concepts could be applied to real-world situations. Rather than treating statistics as a collection of formulas, the course encouraged us to think about why a particular method should be used and what conclusions could reasonably be drawn from the results.

The module reminded me that analysing data was not simply about producing charts, calculating metrics, or building models. It was also about asking the right questions, understanding uncertainty, checking assumptions, and interpreting results carefully.

Statistics plays an important role in analytics because it provides a structured way to evaluate evidence and make decisions under uncertainty. This module encouraged me to become more thoughtful about how I analysed data and communicated insights to others.

Agentic AI for Intelligent Autonomy

During the special term from November to December 2025, I took a five-week course on Agentic AI. Compared with the modules in the regular term, this course was shorter and focused on the general concept of AI agents.

Before taking the course, I had heard many discussions about AI agents, but my understanding of them was still quite general. The module gave me a more structured perspective and helped me understand the different components involved in building an agentic system, including prompt design, retrieval, tool usage, workflow orchestration, evaluation, and the responsible use of AI.

Most importantly, the knowledge I gained from the course has been relevant to my current internship at HP. It has helped me understand some of the ideas and terminology used in AI-related projects and has given me a better starting point for exploring how AI agents can be applied in practical settings.

While five weeks were not enough to master such a broad and fast-changing topic, the course sparked my curiosity and motivated me to continue learning through projects, experimentation, and practical experience.

Looking back on my first four months

Looking back on my first regular and special terms at MITB (four months in total), I can honestly say that I was overwhelmed by the workload.

Every module was demanding, with assignments, group projects, presentations, and final examinations. The week before the final exams was filled with project presentations, and there was no study break in between for students to review the course materials.

I had four examinations during the final week, so I spent countless late nights studying, revising, and trying to consolidate everything I had learnt throughout the term.

I once believed that nothing could be more exhausting than my experience at Shopee. MITB proved me wrong. In a different way, it was even more intense, but it was also a meaningful experience that challenged me to grow, adapt, and discover how much more I was capable of achieving.

The first four months were far more demanding than I had expected, but they also reminded me why I chose this path in the first place. Growth is rarely comfortable, and learning something new requires patience, discipline, and persistence.

My MITB journey is still ongoing, and I know that more challenges are waiting for me. However, with every term, every project, and every late-night study session, I feel that I am moving one step closer to the person I want to become.
